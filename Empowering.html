Dimension 01 • Secular Civic Laws

Constitutional & Civic Rights in Mauritius

Understanding your protected freedoms as a Mauritian citizen under national legislation.

S.11 Constitution of Mauritius

Freedom of Conscience & Religious Practice

Section 11 guarantees every citizen the right to freedom of thought, conscience, and religion. This includes the right to manifest faith in public or private through worship, teaching, practice, and observance.

  • Religious Attire (Hijab/Headscarf): Legally protected in public schools, tertiary institutions, and government workplaces.
  • Freedom of Assembly: Unrestricted right to assemble for daily and Friday (*Jummah*) prayers without state hindrance.
S.3 Protection Against Discrimination

Protection from Discrimination & Creed Bias

Section 3 establishes fundamental rights without discrimination based on race, place of origin, political opinions, color, creed, or sex.

  • Equal Access: Guarantees equal access to public employment, housing, legal representation, and judicial proceedings.
  • Legal Recourse: Victims of religious bias can petition the Equal Opportunities Commission (EOC) or Supreme Court.
Workers' Rights Act 2019

Workplace Rights & Religious Leave

Mauritian labor legislation grants reasonable accommodations for religious observances and statutory leaves for major festivals.

  • Jummah Break Accommodations: Employees can formally request extended lunch breaks for Friday prayers under workplace flexibility rules.
  • Eid Public Holidays: Statutory leave guaranteed for official national holidays including Eid-ul-Fitr.
Consumer Protection Act

Halal Certification & Consumer Rights

Protection against deceptive labeling and false advertising regarding Halal slaughter and dietary standards in commercial trade across Mauritius.

  • Authentic Certification: Ensuring products claiming Halal status carry accredited certifications (e.g., Jummah Mosque Halal Committee).
  • Misrepresentation Claims: Right to report false Halal labeling to the Ministry of Commerce & Consumer Protection.
Dimension 02 • Family & Personal Law

Nikah Registration & Mauritian Civil Code

Harmonizing religious marriage (*Nikah*) and inheritance (*Wasiyyah*) with Mauritian Civil Law.

Nikah & Civil Status Act Registration

الزواج الشرعي والمدني

Under the **Civil Status Act of Mauritius**, a purely religious *Nikah* solemnized without an authorized Civil Status Officer or registered Muslim Civil Status Officer does not automatically confer civil legal protections to the spouse or children regarding inheritance, survivor benefits, or civil maintenance.

Mandatory Steps for Full Legal Protection
  1. Ensure the officiating Imam is an officially appointed Muslim Civil Status Officer in Mauritius.
  2. Publish marriage banns at the local Civil Status Office 10 days prior to solemnization.
  3. Verify that two adult witnesses sign both the civil register and the Islamic *Nikah-Nama*.
  4. Obtain the official Civil Marriage Certificate to secure civil inheritance and estate rights.

Islamic Wills (*Wasiyyah*) & Civil Code

الوصية والميراث

The **Mauritian Code Civil (Napoleonic Framework)** enforces the concept of "Réserve Héréditaire" (statutory reserved share for direct heirs). To execute a Shariah-compliant estate distribution without violating Mauritian law:

1. Non-Heir Bequest Limit (Max 1/3)

Islamic Law permits bequeathing up to 1/3 (*Wasiyyah*) of total wealth to non-statutory heirs or charities. This fits within the disposable portion (*Quotité Disponible*) under the Code Civil.

2. Notarized Mutual Consent Framework

Heirs can execute a notarized agreement (*Acte Notarié*) during estate settlement to voluntarily redistribute shares in accordance with Islamic *Mirath* principles.

Dimension 04 • Organizational Stewardship

Local Society & Waqf Governance (Mauritius)

Statutory compliance, property management, and financial integrity for Islamic associations.

Registrar of Associations Act

Every Muslim Society or Mosque Committee in Mauritius must comply with mandatory statutory obligations:

  • Filing annual audited financial statements before statutory deadlines.
  • Holding democratic Annual General Meetings (AGM) with verified voter lists.

Board of Waqf & Asset Protection

Managing endowed properties (*Waqf*) according to sacred intent and Mauritian legal framework:

  • Registering all real estate endowments with the official Waqf Board.
  • Ensuring commercial rent from Waqf properties matches fair market valuations.

Cash Controls & Internal Audit

Preventing mismanagement and upholding public trust (*Amanah*) through strict cash controls:

  • Dual-custody verification for all Friday collection box (*Lequete*) counts.
  • Mandatory bank deposits instead of holding unrecorded cash reserves.
